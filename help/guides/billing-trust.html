1 · The big idea (read this first)

The portal is set up trust-first. That means:

  • All client money comes into the trust account. Retainers, invoice payments, card/ACH payments — everything lands in trust first.
  • Invoices are billed against trust funds. When you do work, you invoice for it, then the earned money is moved out of trust and into the operating account.
  • The actual bank transfer is done by the firm at the bank. The portal does not move money between bank accounts. What it does is keep the official ledger — a permanent, tamper-proof record of every dollar in, every dollar out, and which client it belongs to.

Think of the portal as your IOLTA-compliant book of record, plus the tool that creates invoices and payment links. The bank is where the money physically moves; the portal is the paper trail a bar auditor would want to see.

Two questions the system answers at any moment
  1. How much trust money does each client have right now?
  2. For every dollar that left trust, which invoice justified it?

2 · Vocabulary (so we all mean the same thing)

TermWhat it means here
Trust accountThe IOLTA bank account where all client funds are held before they're earned.
Operating accountThe firm's own money — where earned fees end up after they're released from trust.
Trust ledgerThe running list of every trust transaction (deposits, releases, etc.). Permanent — nothing can be edited or deleted.
DepositMoney going into a client's trust balance (retainer, invoice payment).
Disbursement / ReleaseMoney coming out of trust — earned fees moving to operating. Always tied to an invoice.
InvoiceA bill for a client (time, expenses, admin fee). Status: Draft → Sent → Paid (or Void).
MatterA client's case. Trust balances are tracked per matter, not just per client.
RetainerThe up-front money a client pays into trust that you draw against as you work.

3 · One-time setup

Configured once per firm and rarely changed.

a) Billing rates

Rates come from the signed client agreement, entered on the client's card — Clients → client → Financial tab → Billing Rates & Admin Fee. (This editor used to live under Settings; it moved to the client card so rates are managed where the client is.)

  • Rates are per-client and per-person. On the same client, the lead attorney can bill $500/hr while a second attorney bills $325/hr and a paralegal bills $175/hr.
  • You can also set a role default (e.g. "any Paralegal on this client = $175") as a fallback for anyone without a specific rate.
  • These rates turn "2 hours of work" into a dollar amount on an invoice.

b) Monthly administrative fee

If the firm charges a standing monthly administrative fee, add it to each invoice as an “Admin Fee” expense line when you bill — Billing → + Add Expense, category Admin Fee. Nothing happens automatically, which means the fee only ever appears on an invoice a person reviewed.

c) Trust account

The firm's IOLTA account (bank name, last 4 digits, jurisdiction) is registered once. All trust activity posts against it.

4 · The day-to-day billing workflow

The main loop the team runs over and over. It lives on the Billing page.

Time & expenses  →  Compose invoice in FreshBooks (leave as Draft)  →  Portal: Attach pay link & send  →  Get paid  →  Release from trust

Step 1 — Work gets logged as time

Time entries flow in from the firm's time-tracking stack (Memtime → FreshBooks). Each entry is matched to a person and a client and priced using that client's rate sheet. Unbilled time waits on the matter until you invoice it.

You can also log time directly in the portal: on the New Invoice screen, + Add time entry takes a description, who did the work, the date, and the minutes (e.g. 7 minutes rounds up to 0.2h at the firm's 6-minute increment). It's priced from the client's rate sheet automatically and lands in the unbilled list, pre-checked for the invoice you're building.

Time logged in FreshBooks as Unbillable still comes across so it can appear on the invoice for the record — but it's flagged “No charge” at $0, so including it doesn't add to the total. Portal-logged entries have the same option: tick No charge and the line shows on the invoice at $0.

Step 2 — Compose the invoice in FreshBooks (and stop at Draft)

Build the invoice in FreshBooks exactly the way you always have — pull in the billed time, add expenses, apply discounts or adjustments, tweak line items. Everything FreshBooks can do on an invoice is fair game, because the portal simply mirrors the finished numbers.

Then stop. Leave it as a Draft. In this workflow, a draft in FreshBooks means "ready to send from the portal."

The one golden rule

Never send an invoice from inside FreshBooks — not the Send button, not "mark as sent." FreshBooks' own email has no payment link in it. The portal is what sends the invoice with the secure pay link attached — and it marks the invoice Sent in FreshBooks for you.

Client already has money sitting in trust? Send the invoice normally, then use Pay from Trust (Step 4, Path A) once it's sent. Hold off on applying credits inside FreshBooks before sending — support for recording the matching trust release is coming.

Step 3 — Send it from the portal (one button does everything)

On the portal's Billing page, open From FreshBooks. Every unpaid FreshBooks invoice appears — drafts wear a Draft badge. For each one:

  1. The matter is matched automatically when the FreshBooks client's email matches the portal's; otherwise pick it from the list.
  2. Click "Attach pay link & send."

That one click does all of this:

  • Copies the invoice into the portal's Billing records — so the trust ledger and the client's own portal view stay complete.
  • Creates a secure payment link that deposits to trust, and writes it into the FreshBooks invoice notes.
  • Has FreshBooks email the client the invoice — PDF attached, pay link included — and marks it Sent in FreshBooks.
  • Saves the invoice PDF into the matter's Files, so it's viewable from the portal, not just FreshBooks.
If the client already paid part of the invoice in FreshBooks, the pay link only requests the remaining balance — never the full amount again.

Alternative — the portal can still compose invoices itself

The FreshBooks-first loop above is the recommended day-to-day path. The portal's own invoice builder still works exactly as before if something ever needs billing without FreshBooks. On the Billing page, click + New Invoice:

  1. Choose the matter (client + case).
  2. The screen shows all unbilled time entries and any unbilled expenses for that matter.
  3. Check the items you want to bill (Select-all is available).
  4. Optionally set a description and due date, then Generate Invoice.

The invoice is created as a Draft. Nothing has been sent to the client yet, and no money has moved.

Reviewing portal drafts (the staging pool)

Drafts collect in the Billing page's Pending review tab (the count shows how many are waiting). This is the review stop before anything goes to a client — open a draft and you can:

  • Edit the header — description and due date.
  • Remove a line (✕) — the time entry or expense goes back to unbilled, so it isn't lost; it'll be offered again on the next invoice.
  • + Add items — pull in more of the matter's unbilled time and expenses, or log a quick manual time entry right there.

The invoice total updates as you edit. You can't empty an invoice or edit one that's already been sent — and you can still Void a draft that shouldn't exist at all.

Sending a portal-composed invoice

Open the draft and click Send Invoice. This:

  • Marks the invoice Sent.
  • Generates a payment link the client can use to pay by card/ACH (this routes to trust).
  • Records the invoice in FreshBooks, and FreshBooks emails it to the client — with the invoice PDF attached and the payment link in the notes. The client's email in the portal must match their email in FreshBooks for this to work.

You can Resend to generate a fresh link and email, or Void if needed.

Clients also see their sent and paid invoices on their own portal — an Invoices card on their Overview tab with a Pay now button and a printable PDF view. Drafts are never visible to clients.

Step 4 — Getting paid: two paths

There are two ways an invoice gets paid, and the button you'll use differs.

Path A — Client already has trust funds Pay from Trust

The client has a retainer sitting in trust. You've now billed them, so you draw the earned amount out of trust.

  • On the Sent invoice, click "Pay from Trust."
  • Confirm (you can enter a check number for the bank transfer if you have one).
  • The system posts a permanent disbursement to the trust ledger for that matter, citing the invoice, and marks the invoice Paid.
  • You then move that money trust → operating at the bank. The portal recorded the release; the bank makes it real.
The system will refuse this if the client doesn't have enough trust funds to cover the invoice (see §6). That's the IOLTA safety net working.
Path B — Client pays the link by card/ACH Record Trust Release

The client didn't have trust funds and instead paid the invoice using the payment link.

  1. Client pays the link → the payment processor notifies the portal → the money is recorded as a trust deposit and the invoice is marked Paid automatically. You don't have to do anything for this part.
  2. Because that money is now earned (you already billed for it), you release it trust → operating: after you move the funds at the bank, open the paid invoice and click "Record Trust Release." This posts the matching disbursement so trust doesn't show money that's really already been earned and moved.
Rule of thumb
  • "Pay from Trust" = they had trust money; you're drawing it down.
  • "Record Trust Release" = they paid by card into trust; you're logging that the earned money left trust.

Either way the end state is the same: invoice Paid, and one clean release on the ledger tying the money that left trust to the invoice that justified it.

Bonus — Requesting a retainer up front

From a client's Financial → Trust tab, Request Retainer emails a secure link that deposits straight into trust (no invoice needed). Two handy options:

  • Open amount. Tick “Let the client enter the amount” to send a link with no fixed amount — the client pays what they can (e.g. you ask for $5,000, they pay $1,000). Whatever they actually pay is what posts to trust.
  • Send to a guarantor. If a parent or someone else is paying on the client's behalf, add them under the client's Other People section (Client tab), then pick them in the “Send to” box on the retainer request.
While a retainer request is out but unpaid, the client shows an amber “⏳ … requested” tag on the Clients list — so you can see at a glance who's been asked for money and hasn't paid yet. It clears automatically when the payment comes in.
When a retainer is paid — two things happen
  • The whole team gets an email. Every staff member receives a "Retainer paid" notice with the client, matter, and amount — no need to go check the ledger.
  • Record it in FreshBooks as a Prepayment credit — never as an invoice. In FreshBooks: Credits → New Credit → Prepayment, under the client, for the amount paid. (Entering it as an invoice makes the client look like they owe that money.) The team email includes this reminder. The portal can eventually do this step automatically — that switch stays off until it's been verified against the firm's live FreshBooks account.

5 · Expenses (hard costs)

Postage, copies, filing fees, etc. that you front on a matter and bill back.

  • Log one from the Billing page (+ Add Expense) or from the client's card (Financial → Trust tab → Expense). Pick the matter, date, category (Postage, Copies, Filing fee, Mailing, Admin Fee, or Other), amount, and description.
  • It shows up in the Expenses register as Unbilled.
  • Next time you make an invoice for that matter, the unbilled expense is offered as a line item — check it to include it. Once billed, it's marked Billed and linked to the invoice.
  • Unbilled expenses can be deleted; billed ones cannot (they're part of an invoice).

Expenses are reimbursed out of the client's trust funds, same as earned fees.

6 · The Trust Accounting page (the book of record)

The compliance heart of the system. It shows:

  • Per-matter trust balances — how much each client/case has in trust right now, computed live from the ledger.
  • The trust ledger — every deposit and disbursement, in order, with a running balance after each entry.
  • Reconciliation — the three-way check (bank statement vs. firm ledger vs. sum of all client balances). The system computes whether all three match; you enter the bank statement figure from the actual statement.

What the system will NOT let you do (on purpose)

These guardrails exist because IOLTA violations mean real liability — the portal enforces them at the database level so no one can get them wrong:

  1. A client's trust balance can never go negative. You cannot spend one client's money on another. An attempt to over-draw is rejected outright.
  2. You can't release money for an invoice that hasn't been sent. The client has to be billed first.
  3. Ledger entries can never be edited or deleted. If something is wrong, you post a correcting adjustment entry — the full history stays intact for an auditor.
  4. One release per invoice. You can't accidentally release the same invoice's funds twice.
If you ever hit an error about "insufficient trust balance" or "invoice not sent," that's the system protecting the firm — not a bug.
