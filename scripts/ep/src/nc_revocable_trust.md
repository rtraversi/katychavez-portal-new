# THE {{principal_name_upper}} REVOCABLE LIVING TRUST

This Trust Agreement is made on ______________________ by {{principal_name}}, of {{principal_address_inline}} (the "Grantor"), who also serves as the initial trustee (the "Trustee").

## Article 1: Name and Establishment

1.1 The Grantor creates this trust, to be known as **The {{principal_name}} Revocable Living Trust dated ______________________**. Title to trust property may be held as "{{principal_name}}, Trustee of The {{principal_name}} Revocable Living Trust dated ______________________, and any amendments."

1.2 The Grantor transfers the property listed on Schedule A to the Trustee, who accepts it and holds it under this Agreement. Property may be added later by the Grantor or by any other person, by lifetime transfer, will, beneficiary designation, or otherwise, if the Trustee accepts it.

## Article 2: Revocation and Amendment

2.1 While living, the Grantor may revoke this trust in whole or in part, or amend it, at any time by a signed writing delivered to the Trustee. This trust is revocable under N.C.G.S. § 36C-6-602.

2.2 Only the Grantor may exercise these powers. A guardian, agent under a power of attorney, or other representative may not revoke or amend this trust unless the power of attorney expressly authorizes it and the Trustee is satisfied that the Grantor would have approved.

2.3 On the Grantor's death or when this trust becomes irrevocable for any other reason, it can no longer be revoked or amended, except that the Trustee may make the administrative changes allowed by Article 10.

## Article 3: Trust Property and the Grantor's Rights

3.1 The trust property, together with additions and reinvestments, is the "Trust Estate."

3.2 While the Grantor lives and is not incapacitated, the Trustee shall pay to the Grantor, or apply for the Grantor's benefit, all net income and as much principal as the Grantor requests. The Grantor may withdraw any or all trust property at any time.

3.3 The Grantor may use any residence, vehicle, or tangible personal property held in the trust, rent-free.

## Article 4: Incapacity of the Grantor

4.1 The Grantor is "incapacitated" if (a) a court of competent jurisdiction finds the Grantor incompetent or appoints a guardian of the estate or person, or (b) two licensed physicians, one of them the Grantor's attending physician, certify in writing that the Grantor cannot manage property or financial affairs prudently. The Grantor is no longer incapacitated when the same kind of finding or certification says so, or when the court restores the Grantor's rights.

4.2 During incapacity, the Trustee shall pay or apply as much income and principal as is needed for the health, support, maintenance, and comfort of the Grantor and of [any person the Grantor is legally obligated to support / SPOUSE NAME AND DEPENDENTS], in the standard of living the Grantor has been accustomed to.

4.3 If the Grantor is serving as Trustee and becomes incapacitated, the Grantor's service as Trustee ends and the successor Trustee under Article 7 takes over without court action.

## Article 5: Distributions at the Grantor's Death

5.1 **Expenses and taxes.** After the Grantor's death, the Trustee may pay the Grantor's legally enforceable debts, funeral and burial expenses, expenses of administration, and death taxes (except taxes on property passing outside the trust, unless the Trustee elects otherwise), to the extent the probate estate is insufficient or the Executor requests payment. 5.3 **Tangible personal property.** The Trustee shall distribute household furnishings, jewelry, clothing, vehicles, and other tangible personal property (not already given by Schedule B) to the Grantor's [SPOUSE/CHILDREN/OTHER], in shares as they agree. If they do not agree within [90] days after the Trustee's written request, the Trustee shall divide the property by lot, or sell it and add the proceeds to the residue. 5.5 **Alternate takers.** If no beneficiary above is living on the Grantor's death, or the gift otherwise fails, the Residue goes to [ALTERNATE BENEFICIARY OR CHARITY, WITH ADDRESS/EIN], and if none, to the Grantor's heirs at law determined under the NC Intestate Succession Act (Chapter 29) as if the Grantor had died then unmarried.

5.6 **Continuing trust for young or vulnerable beneficiaries.** If any share is distributable outright to a beneficiary under age [25] or who the Trustee reasonably finds unable to manage property (because of disability, addiction, creditor problems, or similar), the Trustee may hold that share in a separate continuing trust. The Trustee shall pay as much income and principal as the Trustee decides is needed for the beneficiary's health, education, maintenance, and support, and shall distribute what remains outright when the beneficiary reaches {{distribution_age}}, or earlier if the Trustee finds the problem has ended. If the beneficiary dies first, the remainder goes to the beneficiary's descendants, per stirpes, or if none, to the Grantor's other descendants, per stirpes. ## Article 6: Survivorship, Slayer Rule, and Divorce

6.1 A beneficiary who does not survive the Grantor by [30] days is treated as having predeceased the Grantor.

6.2 A person who would be barred as a slayer under N.C.G.S. Chapter 31A is treated as having predeceased the Grantor.

6.3 If the Grantor's marriage to a beneficiary is dissolved after the date of this Agreement, provisions for that former spouse are revoked and the former spouse is treated as having predeceased the Grantor, unless this Agreement states otherwise.

## Article 7: Trustees

7.1 **Initial Trustee.** The Grantor serves as initial Trustee, with no bond.

7.2 **Successor Trustees.** If the Grantor resigns, becomes incapacitated, or dies, the following serve in order: (1) {{trustee_1_name}}, {{trustee_1_address_inline}}; (2) {{trustee_2_name}}, {{trustee_2_address_inline}}; (3) {{trustee_3_name}}. If all fail to serve, a majority of adult beneficiaries may appoint a qualified successor, and if they do not, a court of competent jurisdiction may.

7.3 **Resignation and removal.** A Trustee may resign on 30 days' written notice to the Grantor (or, after the Grantor's death or incapacity, to the adult beneficiaries and the next successor). While the Grantor is not incapacitated, the Grantor may remove any Trustee at any time.

7.4 **Co-trustees and reliance.** If two Trustees serve, they act together unless one is unavailable or incapacitated. Third parties may rely on a written statement of any acting Trustee, or on a certification of trust under N.C.G.S. § 36C-10-1013.

7.5 **Compensation and bond.** A Trustee is entitled to reasonable compensation and to reimbursement of expenses. No bond is required of any Trustee in any jurisdiction.

## Article 8: Trustee Powers

8.1 The Trustee has all powers granted by the NC Uniform Trust Code, as amended, and may exercise them without court order. Without limiting those powers, the Trustee may:

- Retain, buy, sell, exchange, lease, or mortgage any real or personal property, including real property in {{principal_county}} County, North Carolina, and sign and record deeds and other instruments.
- Invest and reinvest under the NC Uniform Prudent Investor Act, and retain any asset the Grantor transferred, regardless of diversification.
- Hold property in the name of a nominee, or in unregistered form.
- Borrow money, and pledge trust property as security.
- Settle or compromise claims for or against the trust.
- Employ and pay attorneys, accountants, investment advisers, and other agents.
- Divide or distribute trust property in cash or in kind, pro rata or non-pro rata.
- Access, manage, close, and terminate digital assets and accounts of the Grantor, consistent with N.C.G.S. Chapter 36F.
- Deal with the Grantor's executor, including buying assets from or lending money to the probate estate.
- Pay a minor or incapacitated beneficiary's share to a custodian under the NC Uniform Transfers to Minors Act, or directly for the beneficiary's benefit.

8.2 The Trustee shall act in good faith, in the interests of the beneficiaries, and in accordance with the terms of this Agreement and Chapter 36C.

## Article 9: Spendthrift Provision

9.1 No beneficiary may anticipate, assign, pledge, or otherwise transfer an interest in income or principal before actually receiving it. To the fullest extent N.C.G.S. § 36C-5-502 allows, no interest of a beneficiary is subject to the claims of creditors or to legal process before distribution. This Article does not apply to the Grantor's interest while the trust is revocable.

## Article 10: Administration

10.1 **Accounts.** After the Grantor's death or incapacity, the Trustee shall keep the qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed as N.C.G.S. § 36C-8-813 requires, and shall give each current beneficiary an annual account of receipts, disbursements, and assets.

10.2 **No court supervision.** The trust shall be administered free of continuing court supervision to the fullest extent the law allows.

10.3 **Administrative amendments.** After the Grantor's death or incapacity, the Trustee may, without court approval, amend administrative provisions to comply with tax law or to improve efficient administration, but may not change a beneficial interest.

10.4 **Perpetuities savings.** Unless sooner terminated, any trust under this Agreement ends 21 years after the death of the last survivor of the Grantor and the Grantor's descendants living on the date of this Agreement, and the Trustee shall then distribute the remaining property to the beneficiaries entitled to income, in proportion to their interests.

10.5 **Small trusts.** If a continuing trust's value is so small that continued administration is uneconomical, the Trustee may terminate it and distribute the property to the income beneficiary, consistent with N.C.G.S. § 36C-4-414.

## Article 11: General Provisions

11.1 **Governing law.** This Agreement is governed by North Carolina law, without regard to where any trust property is located or where the Trustee lives, unless a successor Trustee in another state elects to move the trust's principal place of administration under N.C.G.S. § 36C-1-108.

11.2 **Jurisdiction and venue.** The courts of {{principal_county}} County, North Carolina have jurisdiction over any proceeding concerning this trust.

11.3 **Definitions.** "Descendants" includes persons legally adopted before age 18. "Per stirpes" means a divided share passes to the descendants of a deceased beneficiary. Headings are for convenience only.

11.4 **Severability.** If any provision is invalid, the rest of this Agreement remains in force.

11.5 **Counterparts and copies.** Copies of this signed Agreement are as effective as the original.

11.6 **Pour-over.** The Trustee shall accept property passing to the trust under the Grantor's will or by beneficiary designation, and the Grantor's will may refer to this trust under N.C.G.S. § 31-47.
